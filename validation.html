Who it is for, and what it must do

Audience facts come from the live site. Where something is a reasoned assumption rather than a published fact, it is labelled.

The visitor

People in Ballarat with curly, kinky, coily or textured hair, and parents of curly-haired children, who until now travelled to Melbourne because local salons could not cut or style afro hair.

Source: Natacha's own story on the current site, and Claire's quote about finally finding a hairdresser for her daughter.

Their one question

"Can she do my hair, and how do I get in?" The page answers the first in the opening screen (specialist, services, award) and the second with Book and Call buttons that never leave reach on a phone.

Assumption: most visitors arrive on a phone from Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or a local search. Her socials are her most active channel.

The conversion

No online booking system exists. So the page takes a booking request in four short steps and hands it to Natacha pre-written on WhatsApp, SMS or email. It says plainly that she confirms the time.

Source: the live site's only action is "Call Today". Her flyers list WhatsApp on +61 431 024 092.

The brief's hard rules

Real facts only, labelled placeholders, Natacha's own photos, no arch or cutout shapes, a luxurious and readable look, hours with today highlighted, address, map, phone, a sticky Book and Call bar on mobile, and a strong closing call to action.

All rules were checked and are listed in the results below.

Each design decision, and the reason for it

Black-and-white prints that develop into colour

Natacha's style photos are small phone pictures (some only 267 by 248 pixels) with clashing backgrounds: wood doors, pink tops, a yellow stall tablecloth. Printed in black and white inside a bone mat, they read as one consistent, expensive collection. Hover, or tap "Show in colour", and each print shows the real colours, so nothing is hidden.

Cultural reference: J.D. 'Okhai Ojeikere's black-and-white Hairstyles series, which photographed African hairstyles as sculpture.

A dark gallery, with gold kept for prices

A near-black ground with bone-white type gives the quiet, high-end feel the brief asked for. It also lets the hair be the brightest thing on the screen. Gold, the colour of her logo, is reserved for prices and the refrain "we do them all", so the eye goes there.

Measured contrast: bone on black 15.5 : 1, gold on black 8.2 : 1, secondary text 7.2 : 1.

A readable typeface

Archivo is one family that reads clearly at body size and can widen into an elegant display cut for headlines. That answers the request for easier reading without losing the luxury feel. Body text is 17px with generous line spacing.

No visible text on mobile is smaller than 12px. Every text block passed contrast.

Her own words as the headline

"We do them all." is Natacha's own line from her site. It says she serves every texture, heritage and age in four words. The line underneath states plainly what she is and where: afro and curly hair specialist, Black Hill, Ballarat.

Brief requirement: say what this is, why it is worth it, and offer the action, all in the first screen. Checked at 1440, 1400, 800 and 390 pixels wide.

The shop is the one room in colour

Products are recognised by their packaging colours, so the shop switches to a bright paper ground and full-colour photos from her Instagram. Every price is copied exactly from her October 2025 price list, under "from $14".

Source: April 2026 range PDF on her site (prices as at 01.10.2025).

Booking, one decision at a time

One question per step (service, day, details), then a review screen with "Change" links before sending. Each step checks its answer before moving on and explains exactly what is missing. Fewer choices on screen means a faster, calmer decision on a phone.

Tested: 12 booking tests, including empty steps, a one-letter name, Back, Change, the Enter key, and the exact WhatsApp message produced.

The mobile bar waits its turn

On phones, the sticky Call and Book bar slides in only after the opening screen's own buttons scroll away. This avoids five competing buttons in the first view, a problem the version 1 review caught.

Verified on a 390 pixel screen, both before and after scrolling.

Rectangles only

No arches, circles, notches or masked photo shapes anywhere, as requested after version 1. Structure comes from mats, thin rules and spacing instead.

Checked by script: no element on the page has a corner radius above 0.

Placeholders and honest limits

  • Placeholder service prices ($45, $90 and $120 "from") and opening hours are samples, labelled on the page. Natacha must supply the real ones. Neither is published anywhere today.
  • Placeholder the four client quotes are carried over from her current site and labelled for her to confirm.
  • Photo quality is capped by the source. Logged-out Instagram only exposes her 11 newest posts at 640 pixels, and her website photos are small. Full-size originals from Natacha would sharpen every print.
  • This is pre-launch validation. No real visitors have used the page yet, so there is no conversion data. The best next step is to launch and count taps on Book now, Call and WhatsApp.
  • Not covered: manual screen-reader testing, physical iPhone and Android devices, and an actual WhatsApp send. The tests confirm the exact message and link that would open.
  • Speed was measured from a local server. GitHub Pages adds network distance but also compression, so expect similar results.
